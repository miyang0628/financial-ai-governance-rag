### Overview — Japan AI Promotion Act

**Legal status:** Mandatory statute in force, but **promotional in nature** with no penalties. Business operators bear only a best-effort duty to cooperate with government measures. There is no high-risk/high-impact classification and no financial-AI-specific obligation.

**Regulatory model:** Promotion-and-coordination (振興・調整), contrasting sharply with the EU AI Act's and Korea's/Brazil's risk-based mandatory-obligation models. Risk is addressed through existing criminal law and sectoral business acts, government guidelines, and investigation/guidance powers — not through a dedicated AI risk-tier with duties and fines.

**Purpose (Art. 1, paraphrased):** To improve citizens' lives and develop the national economy by comprehensively and systematically promoting research, development, and utilization of AI-related technology, while ensuring transparency for appropriate R&D and utilization.

**Effective date:** Core provisions from 2025-06-04; Chapter 3 (AI Basic Plan) and Chapter 4 (AI Strategic Headquarters) fully effective 2025-09-01. AI Basic Plan adopted by cabinet decision 2025-12-23.

**Key financial AI provision:** **NONE specific.** The Act does not classify credit scoring, lending, insurance, or any financial AI as a special-risk category. Financial AI in Japan is governed by existing sectoral law (Banking Act, FIEA, APPI) and by non-binding government guidelines, not by this Act.

**Comparison with EU AI Act / Korea / Brazil:** Those are risk-based mandatory regimes with high-risk tiers, concrete obligations, and penalties. Japan's Act is a promotional framework with best-effort cooperation duties and no penalties. This is the deliberate contrast that makes Japan a strength-control in the exposure analysis: low pre-training exposure (Japanese-language, recent) but also low regulatory strength.

### Article 1 — Purpose

This Act aims to improve the lives of citizens and develop the national economy by promoting the research, development, and utilization of AI-related technology in a comprehensive and systematic manner, based on the recognition that such technology is important for economic society and national security, thereby maintaining research and development capabilities and enhancing international competitiveness, while ensuring transparency for appropriate research, development, and utilization.

### Article 2 — Definitions

Defines "AI-related technology" and related terms for the purposes of the Act. The Act defines AI broadly as technology relating to the artificial realization of human intellectual capabilities and its application, without establishing operational risk categories.

### Article 3 — Basic Principles

The development and utilization of AI-related technology shall be promoted based on principles including: (1) recognition of importance for economic society and national security, maintaining R&D capability and enhancing international competitiveness; (2) comprehensive and systematic promotion from basic research through utilization; (3) ensuring transparency for appropriate R&D and utilization; and (4) a leadership role in international cooperation and norm-setting.

> Financial-AI relevance: "transparency" here is a promotional principle, NOT an enforceable transparency obligation on financial-AI operators. Do not equate with EU AI Act Art. 13 or Korean Art. 31. ⚠VERIFY.

### Article (Responsibilities of the State and Local Governments)

The State bears responsibility for comprehensively and systematically formulating and implementing measures to promote AI R&D and utilization. Local governments bear responsibility for measures appropriate to regional conditions. Responsibilities are also assigned to R&D institutions, business operators, and citizens, with an emphasis on strengthening coordination among stakeholders.

### Article (Responsibilities of Business Operators)

Business operators (including operators that develop and provide AI-based products and services) shall endeavor to appropriately conduct research, development, and utilization of AI-related technology, and **shall cooperate with measures implemented by the State and local governments**. This is a best-effort cooperation duty, not a specific compliance obligation with defined requirements or penalties.

> Financial-AI relevance: this is the closest the Act comes to an operator "duty." For a bank deploying credit-scoring AI, this Act imposes only a best-effort cooperation duty — NOT documentation, human-oversight, impact-assessment, or transparency obligations of the kind found in the EU/Korea/Brazil frameworks. ⚠VERIFY exact article number and the "cooperate" wording.

### Chapter 3 — AI Basic Plan (人工知能基本計画)

The government shall formulate an AI Basic Plan setting out the basic policy on measures for promoting AI R&D and utilization. The Plan (adopted 2025-12-23, "Trustworthy AI for Japan's Revival") sets directions including becoming the most AI-development-friendly country while ensuring trustworthiness. The Plan is a policy instrument, not a source of enforceable operator obligations.

---

### Chapter 4 — AI Strategic Headquarters (人工知能戦略本部)

Establishes the AI Strategic Headquarters, chaired by the Prime Minister and composed of all cabinet ministers, to promote measures and request necessary cooperation from relevant administrative agencies. The Headquarters coordinates policy; it is not a supervisory/enforcement authority with sanctioning power over AI operators.

> Financial-AI relevance: contrast with the EU's market-surveillance authorities, Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT enforcement, and Brazil's competent authority (Art. 32). Japan has no equivalent AI-specific enforcement body. Maps to the absence of G4 enforcement obligations.

---

### Basic Measures (基本的施策)

The government's basic measures include: promotion of R&D; expansion and sharing of facilities (e.g., data centers) and data; securing human resources and promoting education; participating in international norm-formulation; establishing guidelines in line with international norms; collecting information and investigating cases where rights and interests are infringed; and providing guidance, advice, and public announcements to business operators and citizens.

> Financial-AI relevance: "investigation of infringement cases" plus "guidance, advice, and public announcement" is the Act's soft-enforcement mechanism — reputational (naming) rather than penalty-based. A financial operator misusing AI could face government guidance/announcement, but NOT a fine under this Act. ⚠VERIFY the guidance/announcement provision wording.

---

### Supplementary Provisions — Review Clause

The Act includes a review clause providing that necessary measures will be taken as required, reflecting the intention to revisit the framework as international norms and domestic circumstances evolve.
